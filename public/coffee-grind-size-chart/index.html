Coffee grind size chart for every brewer

Mokha Caffè · 2026-09-11 · 6 min read

Grind size controls how quickly water can pull flavor from coffee. The right setting depends on the brewer, the coffee, and your grinder, so no chart can give you a universal number. Use the texture below as a starting point. Then use brew time, flow, and taste to make the next small adjustment.

The quick rule. If coffee runs too quickly and tastes sour or thin, try a finer grind. If it runs too slowly and tastes bitter, harsh, or drying, try a coarser grind. Change one step at a time.

Quick coffee grind size chart

Brewing method Starting texture What to watch
Espresso Fine, like fine table salt Shot time and even flow
Moka pot Medium-fine, a little coarser than espresso Gentle flow without sputtering
AeroPress Fine to medium, depending on recipe Recipe time and easy, steady pressure
Pour-over Medium-fine to medium Drawdown time and an even bed
Automatic drip Medium Full basket drainage without overflow
French press Coarse, like rough sea salt Clean plunge and balanced taste
Cold brew Coarse Long steep without excessive sludge

Texture comparisons are approximate. Two grinders can use the same number and produce very different particles. Burr wear, calibration, roast level, bean density, and dose can also change the result. Record the setting that works on your grinder instead of trying to copy another machine's dial.

How to adjust by taste

  • Sour, sharp, or hollow: grind finer to expose more coffee to the water. If the brewer is already running slowly, check water temperature and even wetting before going finer.
  • Bitter, harsh, or drying: grind coarser. If the brew is already fast, use a slightly shorter contact time or cooler water for a dark roast.
  • Weak but pleasant: keep the grind and use more coffee. Our coffee ratio calculator helps separate strength from extraction.
  • Strong but pleasant: use less coffee or more water without changing the grind first.

How to adjust by flow and time

For pour-over and drip coffee, a brew that drains much faster than the recipe expects often needs a finer grind. A stalled or muddy bed often needs a coarser grind. Time is a diagnostic clue, not a target to force at the expense of taste.

Espresso magnifies small grind changes. Move one setting at a time, keep the dose steady, and stop if the machine struggles to pass water. Breville's espresso guidance also treats extraction time and flavor as the practical feedback for adjusting finer or coarser.

Method notes

Moka pot

Start medium-fine, but do not use a powdery espresso grind and do not tamp the basket. Bialetti instructs users to fill the basket without pressing the grounds. Our moka pot guide covers heat, filling, and the point at which to stop the brew.

AeroPress

AeroPress recipes cover a wide range of grind sizes because steep time and agitation also change extraction. Use the grind specified by your chosen recipe. If you need excessive force to press, stop and grind coarser next time.

French press and cold brew

Coarse grounds make filtering easier during a long immersion. If the cup is weak, extend the steep or use more coffee before making a large jump finer. Fine dust can pass through a metal mesh even when the main grind is coarse.

Why this page does not list grinder numbers

A number is useful only for one grinder model in one calibration state. A chart that says "setting 12" without naming the grinder creates false precision. Texture, flow, time, and taste transfer between machines; dial numbers do not.
