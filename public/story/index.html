Al-Mokha: the coffee port behind mocha

Mokha Caffè · Published 2026-08-25 · Updated 2026-09-14

Coffee did not grow in Mokha. The port received it from inland Yemen and Ethiopia, then shipped it abroad. From the sixteenth through the nineteenth century, Mokha was a major coffee marketplace, and its name became attached to the coffee traded there.

The brew before the port

Wild coffee plants originated in Ethiopia, while the drink has its roots in Yemen. By the fifteenth century, Sufis in Yemen were using coffee to stay alert during night prayers. It spread from Yemen to Mecca and reached Cairo, Damascus, and Istanbul during the sixteenth century.

The great entrepôt

Al-Mokha, also spelled Mocha or Mukha, sits on Yemen's Red Sea coast. Coffee arrived by caravan and ship from inland Yemen and Ethiopia. English, Dutch, and French companies maintained trading posts in the city, which reached the height of its coffee trade in the early eighteenth century.

By the end of the seventeenth century, Mokha was the main route through which the world's limited coffee supply moved. That position did not last once live plants and commercial cultivation spread outside Yemen.

Mokha did not grow the coffee. It gave the trade a port and a name.

The monopoly breaks

The Dutch obtained live coffee plants from Mokha in 1616. They later established cultivation in Ceylon, southern India, and Java. Gabriel de Clieu brought coffee seedlings to Martinique in 1720, helping coffee cultivation spread through the Caribbean. As production expanded elsewhere, Mokha lost its former position. Aden and Al Hudaydah eclipsed the port in the nineteenth century.

The name remained. The city gave its name to Mocha coffee. A modern caffè mocha is a different thing: espresso and hot milk with chocolate flavoring.

Why we took the name

We chose Mokha because coffee is an agricultural product that travels. Every cup starts with a farm and passes through many hands before it reaches the drinker. We want to name the origin, credit the growers, and document what we can verify.
