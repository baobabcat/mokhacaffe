The moka pot, properly: a practical brewing guide

Mokha Caffè · 2026-08-25 · Updated 2026-09-13 · 3 min read

The moka pot takes its name from Mokha, the Yemeni port also associated with mocha coffee. Alfonso Bialetti invented the Moka Express in 1933. The brewer can make bitter coffee, but grind, heat, and timing all affect the result.

How the pot works

A moka pot is a small pressure brewer. Heating the sealed base raises the pressure and pushes water through the ground coffee into the upper chamber. Typical pressure is about 1 to 2 bar, well below the 9 bar used for espresso. The result is strong, concentrated coffee rather than espresso. A fine grind or pressed coffee can slow the flow. Excessive heat can produce harsher flavors.

The method

  • Water: fill the base with room-temperature water up to the safety valve. Do not cover the valve.
  • Grind: use coffee ground for a moka pot. Bialetti describes the grind as medium to coarse rather than espresso-fine.
  • Dose: fill the basket generously, level the coffee, and do not press it down.
  • Heat: use low heat. Keep the lid open if you want to watch the flow, but stay clear of the hot coffee.
  • Stop: take the pot off the heat when it begins to gurgle. That sound marks the final phase, when steam mixes with the remaining water and can bring harsher flavors into the cup.
  • Serve: pour the coffee soon after brewing.

Use the pot's fill levels

Fill the water to the valve and the basket to the edge without pressing the coffee. These are the levels in Bialetti's instructions for the Moka Express.

Fill to the valve, fill the basket without pressing, and remove the pot when it gurgles.

If the brew goes wrong

  • Slow or stalled flow: check whether the coffee is too fine or was pressed into the basket.
  • Harsh flavor: lower the heat and remove the pot as soon as it begins to gurgle.
  • Leaks or poor flow: check that the rim and filter are clean, the gasket is seated, and the safety valve is clear. Replace a worn gasket or damaged filter plate.

Care

After each brew, discard the grounds and rinse the parts with water. Bialetti says not to use detergent and not to put the aluminum Moka Express in a dishwasher because this can damage the pot and affect the coffee's taste. Let the parts dry separately. Check the gasket, filter plate, and safety valve regularly.
