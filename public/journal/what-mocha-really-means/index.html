What mocha means: port, coffee, drink

Mokha Caffè · 2026-08-25 · Updated 2026-09-14 · 5 min read

Mocha can mean a port in Yemen, coffee traded under that port's name, or a drink made with coffee, milk, and chocolate. Those meanings arrived at different times. The port came first.

1. The port

Al-Mokha, also spelled Mocha, Mokha, or Mukha, is a port on Yemen's Red Sea coast. Coffee did not grow in the city. Traders brought it from inland Yemen and Ethiopia for shipment abroad. Mokha was a major coffee marketplace from the sixteenth through the nineteenth century, and its trade reached a high point in the early eighteenth century. English, Dutch, and French companies maintained trading posts there.

The port eventually lost its position to Aden and Al Hudaydah, but its name stayed attached to coffee. The longer account is on our story page.

2. The coffee

Europeans used Mocha for coffee imported from Arabia. The label was not always precise. Some shipments mixed Yemeni coffee with beans from Abyssinia, and sellers later applied the name to coffee from other origins. Before 1900, however, mocha still usually meant Yemeni coffee rather than a chocolate drink.

3. The drink

A modern caffè mocha is a chocolate-flavored caffè latte. Its basic ingredients are espresso, hot milk, and chocolate, usually cocoa and sugar, chocolate syrup, or solid chocolate.

The path from the old trade name to the drink took time. Coffee and chocolate were combined in Italy after chocolate drinks arrived there in the seventeenth century. In Turin, a coffee, chocolate, and cream drink called bavareisa developed into bicerin in the eighteenth century. The meaning of mocha began shifting around the start of the twentieth century. A published 1920 recipe for "Chilled Mocha" used milk, coffee, and cocoa.

The port named the coffee. The coffee name later moved to a drink made with chocolate.

Our home recipe

There is no single required mocha formula. This version keeps the coffee easy to taste and lets the chocolate supply most of the sweetness.

  • Coffee: a double espresso, or about 60 ml of strong moka-pot coffee.
  • Chocolate: 15 to 20 g of dark chocolate (60 to 70%), finely chopped, or 10 g of unsweetened cocoa with 5 to 10 g of sugar.
  • Milk: 150 to 180 ml, steamed or heated.
  1. Brew the coffee over the chopped chocolate. Wait 30 seconds, then stir until smooth. If you use cocoa, first mix it with a little hot coffee to make a paste.
  2. Pour in the milk. Hold back the foam until the end if you want to finish the drink with it.
  3. Taste before adding more sugar. If the drink is sweeter or heavier than you want, use less chocolate next time.
